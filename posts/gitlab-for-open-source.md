GitLab for Open Source gives accepted projects no-cost access to GitLab Ultimate features, either on GitLab.com or a self-managed instance, plus 50,000 compute minutes calculated at a program-specific cost factor. This is software and CI support, not cash funding.<!--more-->

**Eligibility.** Every project in the applying namespace must use an OSI-approved open-source license. The organization may accept donations to sustain its work, but must not seek profit by selling services, charging for enhancements or add-ons, or by other means. The GitLab group or self-managed instance and its source code must be publicly visible and available. Personal namespaces are not eligible; GitLab says eligible GitLab.com groups must be owned by the applicant and on the Free tier. The license is for approved public open-source projects in the namespace. Limited private projects for sensitive data require prior written permission.

**Application and renewal.** Sign in to the [GitLab Customers Portal](https://customers.gitlab.com/) and submit the program form. GitLab.com applicants verify their group; self-managed applicants must explain why self-management is necessary. After review and approval, GitLab emails instructions to activate the subscription. Membership must be renewed annually through the application process; an unrenewed account is downgraded. The program page recommends starting renewal at least one month ahead. Self-managed applications may be delayed during holidays.

**Limits.** Product support is not included. The 50,000 compute-minute benefit uses a program-specific cost factor, so it is not a general guarantee of 50,000 standard minutes. See the [program requirements and FAQ](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/marketing/developer-relations/programs/open-source-program/), the [program agreement](https://handbook.gitlab.com/handbook/legal/opensource-agreement/), and GitLab's [compute-minute documentation](https://docs.gitlab.com/ci/pipelines/compute_minutes/) for current terms.
