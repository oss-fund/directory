Open Collective is a platform for groups to raise, manage, and disburse money through public budgets and expense workflows. A Collective is the community-facing group; an Organization is the legal entity that can hold funds as its fiscal host. Projects can use an existing host or, if they have the legal and banking capacity, operate independently.

**Benefits.** A hosted project gets a public page and transparent ledger for contributions and expenses. Depending on the host, fiscal hosting can also provide banking, bookkeeping, tax and compliance administration, invoicing, contract support, grants administration, and expense payments. The Open Source Collective (OSC), one host on the platform, additionally lists virtual cards, payroll and benefits, trademark and domain support, and help receiving grants. These services are provided by the host, not by the platform uniformly.

**Eligibility and getting started.** Create an account and a Collective. Open-source software projects can choose to apply to OSC, which requires an active or meaningfully used project, an accepted open-source license, organizational repository ownership, and an applicant authorized to represent the project. OSC reviews applications and may decline projects it considers a poor fit; GitHub verification may approve clearly eligible projects automatically, while manual reviews generally take a few business days. The application is currently available. Other collectives can select a host suited to their mission and location; each host sets its own eligibility, application, and fees. An independent Collective must arrange its own legal status, bank account, taxes, and financial administration.

**Fees and terms.** Open Collective’s current pricing says Collectives are not subject to Organization subscription pricing. A host may charge a separate host fee, usually as a percentage of contributions; published examples vary, and fees can be higher than 10%. OSC currently lists a 10% host fee on incoming funds, with no setup fee, minimum balance, or recurring fee. Payment processor fees are additional and vary by method, currency, and country. Contributors may add a voluntary platform tip. The pricing docs let an Organization switch to a 5% Crowdfunding Fee instead of tips; separately, the Terms of Service say OFiTech may apply a 5% Crowdfunding Fee charged to the Host when tips are not enabled or available. Organization subscriptions are a separate cost for legal entities and hosts using the platform. OSC-hosted contributions are not tax-deductible charitable donations in the US. Review OSC's fiscal sponsorship agreement for its contract requirements; the linked agreement could not be accessed during this review.
